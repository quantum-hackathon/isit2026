Opening Keynotes

Two invited keynote talks from leading experts at the intersection of quantum computing and information theory.

Keynote 1
Dr. DongDong Li
QuantumCTek Co., Ltd.
Industrial advancement of quantum information technology
Abstract: Quantum information technology is transitioning from laboratory demonstrations to industrial deployment across computing, communication, and metrology. Drawing on QuantumCTek's portfolio, we introduce foundational principles including superposition and entanglement. We then present a suite of deployable products, including QKD systems, superconducting quantum computers, cold-atom gravimeters, and single-photon detectors, which are already deployed in networks, cloud platforms, and the field. This talk offers an industry perspective on how these technologies are achieving real-world impact.
Keynote 2
Prof. Xin Wang
Hong Kong University of Science and Technology
Programming, learning, and protecting quantum data
Abstract: Quantum data differ fundamentally from their classical counterparts, leading to new insights and properties in information transmission and computation. In this talk, I will present frameworks for programming, learning, and protecting quantum data, together with information-theoretic limits on their performance. This talk will discuss several examples, including quantum phase processing for transforming eigenphases of unitary operators, quantum neural networks for quantum process transformation using parameterized quantum combs, sample-efficient algorithms for implementing polynomial functions of quantum states, purification algorithms for protecting quantum data, and Hamiltonian recognition for identifying unknown quantum dynamics. These results demonstrate how algorithmic and information-theoretic tools can jointly advance quantum information processing.

Participation

Eligibility &
How to Join

Open to students, researchers, and practitioners worldwide. Whether you're a quantum veteran or just curious — everyone is welcome to apply.

  • ⚡ Team Composition — Teams may consist of three to five members. Please include all team members' names during registration. Individual registrants are warmly welcome — organizers will actively help match individuals into teams to ensure everyone can participate.
  • 🎓 Student Requirement — Each team must have at least two current students enrolled in an academic programme.
  • ◆ IEEE IT Society Membership — For virtual participants, at least one member of the group must be a registered member of the IEEE Information Theory Society at the time of registration.
  • 🌐 Hybrid Format — Participation in ISIT 2026 in person is not required. The hackathon welcomes both remote and in-person participants equally.
  • 💬 Community & Support — Participants will be added to the Slack channel for updates, technical support, and team-matching opportunities. Join #quantum-hackathon-2026.
